Bull Case
For Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL), the most straightforward positive is that a legal challenge targeting one of its most closely watched AI relationships is going away. The lawsuit alleged that Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) violated antitrust law through its integration of OpenAI's ChatGPT into Apple Intelligence features on iPhones and other devices, while X and SpaceXAI claimed the arrangement shut rival AI providers out and helped Apple and OpenAI protect their respective market positions. Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) denied wrongdoing and had argued that its OpenAI integration was not exclusive.


The dismissal means that Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) no longer has to defend those claims in this particular lawsuit. That holds significance because a U.S. judge had allowed the case to proceed in November 2025, rejecting an earlier effort to end it at that stage. The timing is also notable as Apple's broader AI strategy evolves. Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) launched Siri AI in beta this month as part of the next generation of Apple Intelligence. The company says its new Apple Foundation Models were developed in collaboration with Google and its Gemini models, while ChatGPT remains available to users as an extension within Siri.

That doesn't explain why Musk's companies dropped Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) from the lawsuit. But it does show that Apple's current AI strategy is broader than a single OpenAI integration.

Bear Case
The dismissal leaves an important question unanswered: what ended the dispute?

X and SpaceXAI's filing did not explain their reason for dropping the Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) claims and did not disclose whether a settlement had been reached. Apple, Musk's companies, and OpenAI also did not immediately comment to Reuters at the time of its initial reporting.


OpenAI itself said it was not involved in the agreement and did not know its terms. It has asked X to disclose the agreement, arguing that the details could affect the remaining claims and its defense. So investors should be careful about reading the dismissal as a broader legal validation of Apple's AI strategy. The case did not end with a ruling that Apple's conduct was lawful; rather, the plaintiffs simply moved to dismiss their claims against the company.

And the wider dispute isn't over. X and SpaceXAI are continuing their case against OpenAI, meaning the litigation surrounding the original allegations will carry on even without Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) as a defendant.

Conclusion
For Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL), this is a cleaner outcome than continuing to fight the case in court, but the absence of disclosed terms limits what can be concluded from it. Musk's companies accused Apple of using its relationship with OpenAI to restrict AI competition, while Apple denied that allegation and maintained that its ChatGPT integration wasn't exclusive. Now the company portion of the case is ending without the filing explaining what changed.


That removes one lawsuit from Apple's plate just as the company is pushing deeper into generative AI with Siri AI, its own foundation models and outside AI partners. The legal development is therefore fairly simple: Apple (NASDAQ:AAPL) is out of this particular fight. Why it got out, and whether anything changed hands or changed behind the scenes, remains undisclosed to the public.
